Framework

Classification Methodology

OIICS version 3.0 classifies each case across four structures. My process is to read the record fully, identify the injuring event before the outcome, code each structure in order, and document the rationale so the result is consistent and reviewable.

Nature

The physical characteristic of the injury or illness — for example, fracture, amputation, or burn.

Part of Body

The specific body part directly affected by the injury or illness.

Event or Exposure

The manner in which the injury or illness was produced — the primary axis of OIICS analysis.

Source & Secondary Source

The object, substance, or condition that directly produced or contributed to the injury.

Framework: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, OIICS manual v3.0, implemented for 2023 data forward. bls.gov/iif/definitions

Applied Coding

Case Classification Samples

Each case applies OIICS event-or-exposure coding to a documented incident type. Codes are drawn from the BLS OIICS structure as published in the CFOI 2024 results (Table A-9).

Case 1 — Struck by falling object

Incident narrative: A worker on a goods-producing site was struck by an object that fell from above during handling, resulting in a fatal injury.

Event or exposureStruck by falling object
OIICS event code643
Parent categoryContact incidents (6) → Struck by propelled, falling, or suspended object (64)
The injuring event is the falling object making contact, so it codes to 643 under Contact incidents — not to a fall by the worker.

Case 2 — Caught by running powered equipment

Incident narrative: A maintenance worker was cleaning powered equipment that had not been fully de-energized; the worker was caught by a moving part.

Event or exposureStruck by running powered equipment during maintenance, cleaning, testing
OIICS event code652
Parent categoryContact incidents (6) → Struck, caught, or compressed by running powered equipment (65)
The maintenance/cleaning context is captured by subcode 652 within the running-powered-equipment family — specificity matters here.

BLS CFOI 2024, Table A-9. Table A-9, 2024

Case 3 — Roadway collision, oncoming

Incident narrative: A driver was fatally injured in a roadway collision with another vehicle while moving in opposite directions.

Event or exposureRoadway collision with other vehicle(s), moving in opposite directions, oncoming
OIICS event code2613
Parent categoryTransportation incidents (2) → Roadway incidents (26) → Collision with other vehicle(s) (261)
Coded to the most specific level the record supports — the oncoming-direction detail selects subcode 2613 rather than the parent 261.

Observations

Transportation incidents were the most frequent fatal event in 2024, accounting for 38.2 percent of all occupational fatalities. The overall decline was driven largely by a 16.2 percent drop in deaths from exposure to harmful substances (687, down from 820), itself driven by fewer drug and alcohol overdoses (410, down from 512). On the nonfatal side, 2.5 million private-industry cases represent the lowest total in the series going back to 2003.

Quality Practice

Approach to Ambiguity & Quality

Accurate classification depends on consistent judgment when a record is incomplete. My practice is to code what the record clearly supports, code to the most specific level the evidence allows, document any assumption I make, and flag open questions for a senior analyst rather than guess silently. This keeps the dataset consistent and keeps uncertainty visible.

Example: choosing specificity

OIICS codes are hierarchical. A roadway collision can be coded broadly (Transportation incidents, 2) or specifically (oncoming collision, 2613). When a narrative does not state direction, I code to the parent level the record supports (261) and note what additional detail would be needed to assign the subcode — rather than assuming it. Coding only what the evidence supports protects the integrity of the trend data those codes feed.

On process improvement: documenting coding decisions as I go makes patterns easy to review, supports consistency across a team, and creates a record that helps refine guidance over time.
